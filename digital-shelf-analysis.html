Digital Shelf Analysis

Gardner Bender on HomeDepot.ca

Growth is real, but it’s fragile.

A personal e-commerce measurement project. I looked at 12 months of visits, orders, sales and ratings across Gardner Bender’s 20-SKU portfolio on HomeDepot.ca, and read the listings the way I would read a media plan: what is working, what is not, and what to fix first.

  • Personal project
  • E-commerce measurement
  • 20 SKUs
  • 12 months of data

The project

The headline numbers looked healthy. Online sales were up 10.3% year on year, so the easy read was that the portfolio was growing. I wanted to check whether that growth was real demand, or something thinner underneath.

It was thinner. Conversion was flat at 0.84%. The growth came from dollars per visitor, not from more shoppers deciding to buy. That kind of growth holds only as long as the basket does, which is why I call it fragile.

So the project became a full read of the digital shelf: every SKU, every category, the listings themselves, and a plan built only on the levers a brand advocate actually controls.

What I looked at

  • 1.Categorical overviewHow the three categories compare on visits, orders, sales and ratings.
  • 2.SKU performanceWhich of the 20 products carry the portfolio, and which ones drag it.
  • 3.High-traffic reworkPages that get the visits but not the orders, and what they need first.
  • 4.Product listing auditA live audit of the brand’s public product pages: content, findability and trust.
  • 5.Defending declining SKUsWhich falling products are worth defending, and which are not.
  • 6.A 12-month planBuilt on levers a brand advocate controls: content, findability, and trust.

What I found

  • Online sales were up 10.3%, but conversion was flat at 0.84%. Growth came from dollars per visitor, not demand.
  • Half the SKUs under-convert against the portfolio average.
  • Lifting them all to that average is worth $315,740 a year, about 1,000 extra orders.
  • The fix is not more traffic. It is better content, better findability, and more trust on the pages that already get visited.
