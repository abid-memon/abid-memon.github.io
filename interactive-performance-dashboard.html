Interactive Performance Analytics Dashboard

An interactive dashboard for email marketing analytics, designed and built in Google Looker Studio. On my home page it goes by a shorter name: the Dynamic Dashboard.

Google Looker Studio Email marketing analytics Interactive reporting

The project

What this dashboard is

Email marketing produces a steady stream of results, and reading them usually means opening separate reports, exports and campaign summaries, then piecing the picture together by hand. I designed and built this dashboard to bring that reading into a single place: a live, interactive view of how email campaigns are performing, built in Google Looker Studio.

The idea is simple. Instead of a static report that answers only the questions someone thought to ask when they made it, the dashboard lets the reader ask their own questions. Controls on the page change what is shown, so the same dashboard serves a quick check on recent sends and a slower look at how a campaign has been trending, without anyone rebuilding a report in between.

It is built for the way performance conversations actually go. A result raises a follow up question, and the follow up is usually where the useful answer sits. With the filters and views in one place, that next question takes a click rather than a new request, and the discussion stays on what the results mean instead of how to get them.

Inside the dashboard

What it lets you see

Everything in the dashboard is there to make email performance easy to read at a glance, and easy to question when something looks off.

  • Campaign performance at a glance

    The headline measures for email activity sit up front, so the state of recent sends is clear before any digging starts.

  • Filtering that follows your question

    Controls narrow the view by campaign and by time period, and every chart on the page responds, so the whole dashboard is always talking about the same slice of activity.

  • Trends over time

    Performance is shown as it moves, making it easy to spot whether results are building, holding steady or slipping, and to connect a change to when it happened.

  • Comparisons between campaigns

    Sends sit side by side in the same view, so stronger and weaker campaigns stand out against each other rather than being judged in isolation.

  • Engagement in detail

    Beyond the headline view, the dashboard breaks down how audiences responded to what they received, so interest and drop off are both visible.

  • A shared link, always current

    The dashboard lives at a single address and reflects the connected data, so anyone opening it sees the same up to date picture.
